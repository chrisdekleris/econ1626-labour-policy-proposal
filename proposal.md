# Watching for the First Signal: A Trigger-Based Response to AI in the Australian Labour Market

**Prepared by:** Chris · ECON1626 Economics of Artificial Intelligence · Assessment 2

This brief advises the Deputy Secretary, Employment and Workforce, Department of Employment and Workplace Relations (DEWR 2026c), on extending the Office of the Chief Economist's AI monitoring framework before its quarterly updates resume in late 2026 (DEWR 2026a). It also informs the department's input to the Joint Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence, which reports by 30 November 2026 (Parliament of Australia 2026).

## Executive Summary

## 1. Problem Definition and Context

Theory alone cannot sign AI's net effect on jobs. Automation displaces workers from tasks, while productivity gains and newly created tasks pull labour back in (Acemoglu & Restrepo 2019). DEWR's framework uses the same decomposition, which leaves the answer to the data. In Australia, exposure is broad but automation risk is narrow: about 4% of workers are in occupations with high automation exposure and 79% in occupations with low exposure, with the risk concentrated in routine clerical and administrative roles (JSA 2025).

A worker's loss is not automatically a market failure (Berg 2026). The problem this brief addresses is mainly one of **adjustment and insurance**: skills can lose value faster than workers can retrain, and few workers can insure against that risk privately. A secondary **information** problem justifies public action on monitoring, because timely labour-market evidence is a public good that no single firm has reason to produce.

The current framework has four gaps. It measures employment only, not wages or tasks within jobs. Its survey sample cannot split occupational trends by age. It publishes nothing until late 2026 while the ABS changes its occupation classification (DEWR 2026a). And no policy response is attached to any result. Because new technologies diffuse slowly, finding no effect yet is what we would expect, so it should not be read as reassurance.
